## Interpretation and next step

The [warning feasibility review](warning_evaluation_feasibility.md) and subsequent development analyses distinguish independent later outcomes from direct rule checks. A full controlled warning evaluation still needs independently defined positive and negative events. An injected change is a setup, not proof the intended failure actually occurred. Test-data findings must not be used to tune thresholds.

Different training and evaluation populations can change performance, so the completed score-blind study cannot stand in for a test of a specific split. The 2021 [WILDS benchmark paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/koh21a.html) illustrates this distinction; it is background, not evidence about CERA's results. The [scikit-learn metric documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/model_evaluation.html) defines classification metrics in terms of known labels and predictions; independent event definitions remain a scientific design task.

The existing [warning design](warning_checks_design.md) and [development findings](warning_checks_findings.md) provide boundaries, saved evidence and examples, but explicitly do not define independent ground-truth events. Some checks describe the condition itself: for example, a batch's measured sequence difference is also used by the diversity warning. Testing agreement with that same calculation establishes implementation correctness, not accuracy at detecting a separate harmful loss of diversity. Likewise, limiting initial variants to two substitutions or shrinking calibration data does not ensure the intended failure occurred. A meaningful assessment must distinguish these cases from independent downstream events and report when available data cannot establish them.

## Saved evidence for remaining metrics

Reviewed 2026-11-17 against the main evaluator and a representative saved repair record; this is an availability assessment, not a calculation across all runs. Per-run curves contain the best observed score, eligible-pool simple regret and the top-1% hit indicator at each recorded measurement count. Summaries contain actual measurement counts, covered and total range observations, mean width, warning and repair counts, elapsed time and the later-coverage recovery status. The compressed records contain selected identifiers, measurements, prediction ranges for predictor methods and recorded warnings. Random selection does not have prediction ranges.

- **Top-1% recall:** [post-result findings](additional_metrics_findings.md) now report recall with cutoff ties and distinguish initial and new discoveries. This differs from the earlier yes/no hit. [Recall](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.recall_score.html) measures the fraction of a defined positive set found.
- **Cumulative regret:** [additional metrics](additional_metrics_findings.md) report the sum for new measurements and the per-measurement mean. A sum over fewer measurements can be smaller simply because a method stopped earlier. The optimization literature distinguishes [cumulative and simple regret](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v89/sen19a/sen19a.pdf).
- **Batch ranking and diversity:** [additional metrics](additional_metrics_findings.md) report selected-batch rank correlation and pairwise sequence differences. Constant values, short batches and missing predictor ranges remain explicitly unavailable.
- **Rollback and abstention:** saved repair attempts and stop reasons support counts of retained proposals and stops after failed repairs. The implementation retains the previous rule when a proposal fails verification; do not automatically equate that with reverting a previously accepted rule after later outcomes. Denominators and the intended meaning of rollback require explicit definitions.
- **Detection accuracy and post-failure improvement:** warning counts and later measurements alone do not provide independent failure labels or a causal repair effect. Early stopping also leaves some follow-up unavailable. New controlled designs or justified outcomes are needed; these claims cannot be filled in by renaming existing counts.

These additional calculations are post-result and exploratory. Detection accuracy and post-failure causal effects remain unestablished.

## Periodic retraining comparison

The existing greedy baseline refits the five-member predictor before every batch and selects by predicted mean without reward repair. The fixed-reward baseline refits on the same schedule with fixed reward terms; warnings are recorded, but do not change its selection rule or stop it for failed repairs. Both therefore embody periodic retraining without diagnostic intervention, with different fixed selection rules. They are not a distinct lower-frequency-retraining experiment; adding one would change retraining frequency and confound the comparison with repair. See the [development protocol](matched_reward_comparison_protocol.md) and [study findings](multiprotein_study_findings.md).
