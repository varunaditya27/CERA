CERA | MotivationResearch question

Why can a fitness model lead search astray?

A protein variant is a changed protein. Its fitness is a measured property, such as activity or stability.

1

Predict

A model estimates each variant’s fitness.

→
2

Select

Search favors a high predicted score, called the reward.

→
3

Measure

The selected variant may perform worse than predicted.

Our question: Can that mistake help us change the reward and improve future selections?

We will simulate this using published measurements. No new laboratory experiments are planned.

CERA | MethodRetrospective protocol

How we plan to run an experiment

Use stored measurements to simulate a limited number of laboratory queries.

1

Start

Give the models 100 measured examples.

2

Predict

Train predictors on numerical sequence representations from ESM-2.

3

Select

Use the reward to choose 10 variants.

4

Reveal

Show their stored scores and check for failures.

5

Repair

Check an allowed reward change, then continue or stop.

Budget: 100 starting scores + up to 10 rounds of 10 queries = up to 200 scores.

LLM role: help specify or select an allowed change. A rule-based check rejects changes outside the approved options.

All comparison methods receive the same starting data and budget. Each setting is planned for 10 random seeds.

CERA | MethodFailure and response

How the method responds to a problem

A counterexample is evidence that the current reward or its rules are failing. The proposed responses still need to be tested.

Warning sign
Possible response
A variant breaks a limit
Reject it and correct the filter.
Search moves far from known variants
Limit the distance or penalize distant variants.
Prediction ranges miss too many scores
Update ranges, retrain or reduce the weight of uncertain predictions.
Prediction models disagree
Use a more cautious combined prediction.
Selected variants are too similar
Choose a more varied batch.

Check before continuing: Test a change using known measurements. If it fails, retain the previous reward. Stop after two failed attempts in a row.

CERA | EvaluationComparison plan

How we will compare the methods

Methods for comparison

  • Random: choose variants at random.
  • Greedy: choose the highest predicted scores.
  • Genetic and AdaLead-style search: explore variants near promising ones.
  • Bayesian optimization: balance predicted fitness with uncertainty.
  • Simpler fixes: use a fixed uncertainty penalty or retrain periodically.

What we will measure

  • Best measured fitness found within the budget.
  • Whether prediction ranges contain the actual scores.
  • How often problems are detected and how quickly search recovers.
  • Runtime and number of LLM calls.

We also plan to remove individual parts of our method, such as the LLM or verification step, to see what each adds.

No results yet. A fair comparison is needed before claiming that repair improves search.

CERA | EvaluationLimits of the protocol

What could make the test misleading?

Seeing answers too early

All scores are present in the files today. The search must see only the starting scores and the scores it has requested.

A separate evaluator can use hidden scores to calculate final results, but must not give them to the search or use them to tune it.

Assuming the test is a real lab

The low-to-high split uses measured fitness to form its groups. That is useful for a retrospective test, but a real experiment would not know those scores in advance.

Prediction ranges aim to include the true score 90% of the time. As search chooses new kinds of variants, that rate may change and must be checked.

Clean files are a starting point. They do not prove that the method works.
