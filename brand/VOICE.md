# CERA voice contract

Version: 2026-11-16.

CERA is a research project. Communicate plainly, with confidence proportional to the evidence.

## Rules

- Text must perform a job: orient, explain a concept, label a measurement, provide a choice, or disclose a limitation.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs: "Compare methods", "View results", "Measured fitness", "Repair rejected".
- Headings should be short and substantive. One clear central sentence is better than several slogans.
- Make a distinction between illustrative visualizations, current experiments, and future evaluation.
- Use "development result" when reporting exploratory amylase runs.
- If the outcome is negative, say what happened without softening it.

## Avoid

AI hype, invented confidence, repeated taglines, overlong eyebrow labels, all-caps filler, "revolutionary", "next-gen", "unlock", "seamless", "intelligent", "transformative", "elevate", and empty claims of scientific certainty.

Avoid overusing "audit", "evidence" and "research" in adjacent headings. Explain terms only once in the appropriate context. Prefer a technical link over a wall of caveats on the landing page.

## Approved examples

- "When the reward is wrong."
- "Nine methods across ten matched seeds."
- "No proposed repair passed verification."
- "Explore the method."
- "Full study and limitations."

## Hard boundary

The current repair experiment did not demonstrate a successful repair. Never imply the system routinely fixes itself. IRED and hydrophobic-core datasets are prepared but do not yet provide the matched outcomes shown on the landing page.
