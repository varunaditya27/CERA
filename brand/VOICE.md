# CERA / writing and UI copy

The interface should make the work intelligible without turning it into an advertisement.

## Sound like

A careful researcher showing a colleague what happened. Clear, observant, occasionally surprising.

Good:
- "A prediction isn't proof."
- "Predicted 9. Measured 3."
- "Choose a protein. Reveal its score."
- "No repair passed verification."
- "See how the run works."

Avoid:
- slogans repeated between sections
- large blocks explaining terms the reader has not asked about
- claims of AI autonomy or successful repair not borne out by tests
- empty labels such as “intelligence engine”, “next-generation discovery”, “unleashing potential”, “transformative platform”
- repeated meta-lines describing “evidence-first”, “auditable”, or “research” without showing anything

## Gradually reveal complexity

The homepage explains the problem in one sentence and links to a hands-on walkthrough.

The walkthrough uses an **illustrative**, two-round example and shows known versus hidden measurements. One changed step at a time.

The method page is the place for the implementation loop and its limits.

The system page describes what has actually been implemented. It links to code, not decorative architecture cards.

The results page uses exact source-linked figures with a plain statement of what is not yet established.

Dataset profiles retain the necessary biological and provenance terms because the details matter there.

## Rules

- Every sentence should answer a specific question or tell the reader what to do.
- Prefer concrete verbs and direct labels.
- Treat counterexamples as observed mismatches, not proven diagnoses.
- Use “developed” and “prepared” with care; do not imply a completed final evaluation.
- Show the date and source alongside static study results.
- Humor and dramatic phrases do not belong in scientific caveats.
